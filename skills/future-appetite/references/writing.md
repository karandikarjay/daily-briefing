# Narrative writing reference

This fictional example illustrates voice and structure; it is not evidence for publication:

## Hospital trial adds evidence for plant-based defaults

Making plant-based meals the default could help hospitals reduce beef purchases while preserving diners' choices. In a six-month study by the fictional Institute for Food Choice, eight hospitals using that approach bought 18% less beef than comparison hospitals.

The result is promising, but the hospitals weren't randomly assigned, so other differences could explain some of the gap. Researchers also didn't measure food waste or whether diners compensated by eating meat elsewhere.

Name the actual research organization in real copy. Link a natural phrase such as “study by Faunalytics” to the retained source. Integrate attribution and limitations into the narrative. Avoid the recurring bold lead-ins used in many Axios newsletters: Jay likes their concision and polished appearance, but prefers cohesive prose.

A useful question for readers is welcome when it follows naturally from the evidence. Do not assume all readers are grantmakers. An absent detail in a press release does not prove the full research omitted it. Avoid unsupported adjectives such as cost-effective, scalable, transformative, or commercially viable.
